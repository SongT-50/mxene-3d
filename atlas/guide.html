MXene Atlas — start here

An early-stage, evidence-linked map of MXene properties from open-access experimental papers — about 2,600 values from about 650 papers. Each value links to the sentence it came from and to any measurement conditions extracted with it; completeness and accuracy have not yet been confirmed by an expert.

① Map →the main page: filter, read source sentences, ask questions ② Explore mode (3D) →a periodic-table island showing where data exists — and where it does not yet
Status, honestly: values have been checked against their source sentences by two AI judges only — no human expert check yet, so nothing is labelled “verified”. Measurement conditions differ between papers, so values should not be compared directly. Your corrections are exactly what this needs.

How to open

  • Open the links above in any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox); the map also works on a phone, though a larger screen is easier.
  • The 3D network on the Map page loads a public graphics library, so give it a few seconds on the first visit.

① Map (index.en.html) — what you can do

  • Pick a property and unit (capacitance F/g, conductivity S/cm, EMI shielding, surface area, d-spacing, work function, thermal conductivity …). One dot = one sample under one condition.
  • Click any dot to see the original sentence, the measurement conditions and the paper (DOI).
  • Filter by structure: click an element in the periodic table, or a composition (Ti₃C₂, V₂C, Nb₂C …).
  • Follow the 3D network: composition → etchant → delamination → sample form → measured property; thicker lines = more values. Click a paper dot for its sentences.
  • Ask a question such as “high conductivity MXene”, “LiF/HCl vs HF, conductivity” or “non-Ti, high capacitance” — it lists which etchant and form were used for the samples with matching reported values. These are source candidates, not a promise that a recipe gives that value — please check the conditions of each.
  • Find by target value: type the value you need, and see how the samples associated with those reports were made.
  • Sample tabs: “Strict” keeps pure MXene (no dopant, intercalant or composite); “All” shows everything.

② Explore mode (explore.en.html) — what you can do

  • Towers on the periodic-table island show how many values were collected for each composition — height is the amount of data, not material quality.
  • Fog marks compositions with no value yet in the papers collected — not that no value exists. It is a guide to the gaps: where additional papers or measurements could expand the atlas.
  • Property planet: choose a property and a planet rises above the island, with beams to the compositions that have values. Click it for the top values.
  • Click a tower for its source sentences and DOIs; view a simple sketch of its layer stacking; collect findings with Discovery notes and download them as text.
  • “To fill when data arrives” lists questions the atlas could not yet answer for lack of papers.

Feedback that would help most

  • Values or conditions that look wrong — please identify the data point; a screenshot of its source panel is enough.
  • Definitions we should change, e.g. what counts as pure MXene versus composite or intercalated material.
  • Important properties, compositions or papers that are missing.
